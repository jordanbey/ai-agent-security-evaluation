Access Control Policy

All employees must use individual user accounts. Shared accounts are prohibited unless specifically approved for operational requirements.

Access should follow the principle of least privilege. Users should receive only the permissions necessary to perform their assigned duties.

Administrative privileges require manager approval and security team authorization. Administrative accounts must never be used for normal office work, web browsing, or email.

Multi-factor authentication is mandatory for remote access, privileged accounts, and access to sensitive systems.

Managers must review employee permissions every three months. Any unnecessary permissions should be removed immediately.

When employees transfer to another department, their access rights must be reviewed and adjusted to match their new responsibilities.

When employment ends, all logical access must be disabled immediately. Active sessions, API keys, VPN access, physical access cards, and authentication tokens must be revoked as soon as possible.

Service accounts must have a documented owner, a documented purpose, and regularly rotated credentials.

Security teams should monitor repeated authentication failures, privilege escalation attempts, unusual login locations, and unexpected administrator activity.

All access requests and approvals should be documented and retained for audit purposes.

Periodic access reviews help ensure compliance with organizational security policies and regulatory requirements.
